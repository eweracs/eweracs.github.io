# Settings and licences {#settings}

@lede One window for your licence, the tagger’s shortcuts, the advanced options and the release notes.

Choose *Glyph → Italify → Settings and Licences…* – or the same entry in the gear menu of the filter dialogue, which closes the dialogue first, as *Cancel* would. The window has four panes.

## Licences {#licences}

Enter your licence key – `ITFY-` and eight letters and digits, from your licence email – into the field and press *Add*. Case and dashes don’t matter. Entering a key needs an internet connection: Italify registers this Mac with the licence (up to three Macs per seat, one for a trial) and fetches everything on it – master credits, passes, an automatic renewal. A long licence code from an older email works in the same field. The filter dialogue’s *Licence…* button brings you here, too (closing the dialogue – run the filter again once the licence is in).

You enter a key once per Mac. Whatever is added to the licence later – more credits bought with your key, another pass – reaches every Mac on it by itself within a day; *Refresh* fetches it at once.

A time pass starts when you say so. When a pass arrives – with your key, or added to your licence later – it waits, and Italify asks whether to start it; *Start Pass…* here (or in the filter dialogue’s licence bar) starts it whenever you are ready. A pass runs for the whole licence: started on one Mac, it runs on all of them, and a Mac added in the middle of a pass gets the rest of it. A pass started while another is running begins when that one ends, so no day is lost. Starting a pass needs an internet connection. A trial starts as soon as you enter it.

Under the headline, your licence key is shown for copying – to enter it on another Mac, or to quote it when you get in touch. Below the field is your licence history:

- **Licences on this Mac** – everything your licences hold here, with what it granted (master credits, a full-access pass or a trial) and whether it still counts: *Active*, *Until …* for a running pass, *Not started* for a pass waiting for you to start it, *Starts …* for one queued behind the running one, *Ended …* for one that is over.
- **Activated masters** – the masters you have spent a credit on, with their font and the date. An activation belongs to the master itself, so renaming the master or the file changes nothing. A master shown as *Unknown master* was activated before Italify kept names; open its font and the name fills in.

Credits are spent in the filter dialogue, never here: when you run Italify on a master that is not activated yet, the licence bar at the bottom of the dialogue offers *Activate…*.

## Shortcuts {#custom-shortcuts}

Every single-key shortcut of the Italify Tagger can be re-assigned, including the key that activates the tool. The pane works like the Shortcuts list in Glyphs’ own settings: click a shortcut and it turns into a field waiting for input, then press the new key. [[Esc]] – or a click anywhere else – cancels. Inside the field, ↩ puts that shortcut back to its default and ✕ (or [[⌫]]) removes it; an action without a shortcut shows a greyed *Record shortcut*. *Restore Defaults* resets all of them. A key that another tagging action already uses is refused – move or remove that one first.

The right-click menu in the tagger always shows the keys currently assigned. The defaults are listed in the [keyboard reference](shortcuts).

Keep in mind that, while the tagger is active, a key assigned here takes precedence over a Glyphs tool shortcut on the same key: assign [[T]] and it no longer switches to the Text tool from the tagger.

Shortcuts with [[⌘]] – copy, cut, paste, select all – and [[Tab]], [[Esc]], [[⌫]] and [[Space]] are fixed.

## Advanced {#advanced}

Behaviours of the filter that have no control in its dialogue. All default to what most users want; they are described under [Advanced settings](filter#hidden-settings) in the filter chapter. In the tolerance field, [[↑]] and [[↓]] change the value by 1 unit, with [[⇧]] by 5. *Restore Defaults* resets the pane.

*Write a log file* records what the filter does, which helps with bug reports; *Show in Finder* takes you to the file. Leave it off otherwise – the file grows with every run.

## What’s New {#whats-new}

The release notes of every version. After an update, Italify shows the changes since the version you had in a window of their own, once; untick *Show what’s new after an update* if you would rather not see it.
